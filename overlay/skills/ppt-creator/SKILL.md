## Presentation Structure

A good presentation follows this flow:

```
Slide 1: Title Slide
  - Main title
  - Subtitle
  - Presenter name
  - Date

Slide 2: Agenda/Overview
  - What we'll cover
  - Why it matters
  - Brief outline

Slide 3-10: Main Content
  - One idea per slide (max 3-5 points)
  - Use visuals/charts when needed
  - Keep text minimal

Slide 11: Summary/Conclusion
  - Key takeaways
  - Next steps
  - Call to action

Slide 12: Q&A / Thank You
  - Contact info
  - Discussion questions
```

## Content Guidelines

**Text:**

- Keep it short (max 6 lines per slide)
- Use bullet points, not paragraphs
- Font size: 24pt+ for titles, 18pt+ for body

**Design:**

- Use 2-3 colors max (e.g., primary + accent)
- High contrast text on dark backgrounds
- Consistent font throughout
- One image per slide (optional)

**Storytelling:**

- Start with why it matters
- Present evidence/ideas in logical order
- End with clear next steps

## Best Practices

**Before you create:**

- Know your audience (executives, team, customers)
- Define your main message (1-3 key points)
- Gather your data/results beforehand

**During creation:**

- Focus on one main idea per slide
- Use charts for data, not tables
- Keep animations minimal and professional
- End with a clear call-to-action

**After creation:**

- Practice your presentation once
- Get feedback on content and flow
- Adjust based on audience response

## Tips for Better Presentations

1. **Start strong** - Your first 30 seconds determine attention
2. **Tell stories** - Use examples and real cases, not just facts
3. **Use visuals** - Charts, diagrams, and photos make content memorable
4. **Practice** - Rehearse your presentation 2-3 times
5. **Be yourself** - Authentic delivery beats perfect scripts

## Common PPT Mistakes to Avoid

- Too much text on slides (walls of text)
- Low contrast colors (hard to read)
- Inconsistent fonts and sizes
- Poor quality images (blurry, stretched)
- Reading from slides instead of speaking to audience
- No clear structure or flow
- Too many slides (keeps audience waiting)
